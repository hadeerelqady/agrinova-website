Farming for the next harvest, and the ones after it.

In a region where water is scarce and much of the food is imported, how produce is grown and moved matters as much as how it tastes. This is how we approach it on our own farms, with the growers we work with, and on the journey to Dubai.

Feeding the Gulf well means growing and moving food with care for what's left behind.

As a direct operator, we're responsible for more of the journey than a trader would be: the fields we farm, the growers we choose, how produce is handled and how far it travels. That gives us more ways to do it better.

Sustainability for us isn't a separate programme. It's built into how we plan, grow, pack and deliver.

  • Water is the limiting input

    Across Egypt and the Gulf, farming depends on water that is in short supply. Using less of it per crop is the single biggest lever we have.

  • Food security is a national priority

    The UAE's National Food Security Strategy 2051 aims to make the country's food supply more resilient. Reliable regional sourcing and local harvests are part of that.

  • Heat and distance shorten shelf life

    Produce loses quality quickly in Gulf conditions. A short, cold chain protects freshness and cuts what gets thrown away.

Four principles we work by.

These guide decisions on our own farms and the standards we look for in the growers we work with.

Care for the soil

Healthy soil grows better produce, season after season. On our own farms we treat the land as something we hand on, not something we use up.

  • Rotating crops between seasons so the same field isn't asked for the same thing every year
  • Returning organic matter to the land and resting fields when they need it
  • Growing each crop where the soil and climate suit it best

Respect for water

Water is the scarcest input in our region. Every crop we grow is planned around how much water it really needs.

  • Irrigating to each crop's needs and stage of growth, not by the calendar
  • Favouring efficient irrigation methods on our farms
  • Watering in the cooler hours to lose less to evaporation

Shorter supply chains

Every extra handler between farm and buyer adds time, distance and risk. We keep the chain short because we run most of it ourselves.

  • Our own farms and direct relationships with the growers we buy from
  • UAE harvests whenever they're in season, for the shortest journey of all
  • One operator accountable for quality from field to receiving dock

Less waste

The most sustainable produce is the produce that gets eaten. Most food waste happens between harvest and shelf, so that's where we focus.

  • Planning harvests around confirmed orders
  • Keeping the cold chain unbroken from packhouse to delivery
  • Grading carefully so every box goes to the buyer it suits

From field to receiving dock.

Each stage of the journey is a chance to save water, time or waste. This is the path our produce takes.

  1. Plan

    Harvests are matched to buyers' order calendars before the season starts.

  2. Grow

    Crops are grown where they suit the land, with water used carefully.

  3. Harvest

    Picked at the right maturity, so less is lost before it reaches a buyer.

  4. Grade and pack

    Sorted by size and quality and packed to each buyer's specification.

  5. Keep it cold

    Each product held at its correct temperature on the way to Dubai.

  6. Deliver

    From Ras Al Khor to receiving docks across the UAE and GCC.
